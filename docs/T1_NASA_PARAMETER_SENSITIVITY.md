# T1 NASA fact-sheet parameter sensitivity

This optional audit checks how the T1 Hohmann **time-of-flight calculation** changes when its two circular-orbit radii are replaced with rounded, published semimajor axes. It does not change the synthetic T1 training or holdout data, its accepted Run, or the evidence level of the benchmark.

The read-only source capture on 2026-09-27 used the [NASA Earth Fact Sheet](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/earthfact.html) and [NASA Mars Fact Sheet](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/marsfact.html). The Earth page gives 149.598 million km and 365.256 days; the Mars page gives 227.956 million km and 686.980 days, and repeats the Earth comparison values. The captured full-page UTF-8 byte SHA-256 digests, short source-row excerpts, capture time, URLs, and extraction status are in [the snapshot](../artifacts/t1-nasa-factsheet-snapshot.json). Full pages are not committed. The two source-row shapes differ: Earth's orbital table is preformatted text, while Mars uses HTML rows. The verifier rejects duplicate, missing, or differently shaped orbital rows.

The same solar $\mu=132712440018\ \mathrm{km^3/s^2}$ from the **synthetic benchmark fixture** is used on both sides of the comparison. For $a_t=(r_1+r_2)/2$, the two-body half-ellipse time is $t=\pi\sqrt{a_t^3/\mu}$. Substituting the rounded fact-sheet radii yields 258.8832388317415 days, versus 258.8657589178094 days with fixture radii, a difference of 0.0174799139321 days. The source sidereal periods and axes also imply solar $\mu$ values within the predeclared 0.1% relative sanity limit; this is a rough consistency check, not a parameter fit or an independent trajectory solution. The full machine-readable calculations and limits are in [the audit](../artifacts/t1-nasa-factsheet-audit.json).

Replay the **committed short-row snapshot** without network access with `python scripts/verify_t1_nasa_factsheets.py verify` in the pinned environment. The verifier checks row hashes and extracted values, Earth values across pages, broad physical sanity bounds, the Kepler tolerance, the snapshot/script/source hashes, and exact equality to the committed audit. It cannot authenticate the original HTTPS response offline: the full source pages are deliberately absent, so the recorded page hashes are capture metadata rather than a byte-for-byte offline provenance proof. The short-row snapshot can be audited against the linked live pages again if needed, but live pages may change.

These are rounded mean orbital elements, not a dated ephemeris, launch geometry, Lambert solution, perturbation model, or mission validation. A separate [two-body numerical cross-check](T1_EXTERNAL_ORBIT.md) tests propagation within the same idealized assumptions but does not establish a real mission trajectory. The source pages have not received a page-specific data-use and redistribution review. [NASA's general data license guidance](https://science.data.nasa.gov/about/license) does not, by itself, resolve these particular fact sheets' rights. Consequently `source_rights_status=unreviewed-page-specific`, `offline_origin_authentication=false`, `real_data_claim=false`, and `scientific_validation_claim=false` remain mandatory. Dated ephemeris and mission-trajectory comparison and human scientific review are open gates.
