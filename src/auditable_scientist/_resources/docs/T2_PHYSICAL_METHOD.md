# T2 planar collision counterfactual method

This is a deterministic two-dimensional ball-and-floor simulator. It is an algorithmic, dimensioned **synthetic** benchmark. It does not identify causal effects from observational data, establish real-world predictive accuracy, or quantify model discrepancy. The state is $(x,y,v_x,v_y)$ in metres and metres per second. A horizontal impulse $J$ changes the initial velocity by $J/m$. Between impacts, $x'=x+v_x\Delta t$, $y'=y+v_y\Delta t-\frac12g\Delta t^2$, and $v_y'=v_y-g\Delta t$. At a floor impact, the model sets $v_y^+=-e v_y^-$ and $v_x^+=(1-\mu)v_x^-$, with restitution $e$ and *impact-only* horizontal friction $\mu$ in their declared bounds. This is a model choice, not a general friction law. For the committed release-from-rest fixtures, the first impact time is $\sqrt{2h/g}$ and first rebound apex is $e^2h$.

The causal graph is `mass -> impulse-induced vx -> horizontal trajectory`; `impulse -> impulse-induced vx`; `friction -> post-impact vx -> horizontal trajectory`; `restitution -> post-impact vy -> vertical trajectory and impact schedule`; `gravity -> vertical trajectory and impact schedule`. A parameter intervention replaces only named parameters; a policy counterfactual replaces only the impulse. Both branches share the same initial state and sampling grid. Comparing two model outputs therefore measures an effect **inside this specified structural simulator**, not an observational association.

The fixed 100-case suite has 40 training and 60 holdout scenarios, with single-parameter, joint-parameter and policy queries. Required gates: identical no-op trajectory; at least one impact per branch; first-impact and rebound-apex formulas within $10^{-12}$; free-flight energy residual and positive impact-energy gain at most $10^{-10}$ joules; expected mass, friction, restitution, and policy directions at the relevant event; and a zero-effect estimator rejected on holdout with RMSE above 0.01 in the relevant units. An individual joint intervention can have either sign for final horizontal displacement because restitution also changes impact timing, so the friction direction is checked at the first impact. Out-of-domain or invalid interventions fail model validation. The result reports real-world model uncertainty as unquantified and leaves data rights, causal identification, and external algorithm integration open.
